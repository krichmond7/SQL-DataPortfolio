# SQL-DataPortfolio
## Findings

### Finding 1: Satisfaction Scores and Attrition
Employees who left the company reported slightly lower average satisfaction across all three measures — Environment Satisfaction, Job Satisfaction, and Work-Life Balance — compared to employees who stayed (who averaged ~2.77–2.78 out of 4 on each). The gap between those who left and those who stayed was modest rather than dramatic, suggesting that low satisfaction alone is not a strong standalone predictor of attrition. This pointed toward testing more structural factors, like overtime, as a next step.

### Finding 2: Overtime Is a Stronger Predictor Than Work-Life Balance Alone
Attrition rates roughly doubled or tripled for employees working overtime, regardless of their reported Work-Life Balance score. Employees with a "good" Work-Life Balance rating (3) who did not work overtime left at a rate of just 8.45%, but that rate jumped to 28.74% among employees with the same rating who did work overtime. The pattern held across nearly every Work-Life Balance level, and even employees reporting the best possible Work-Life Balance (4) still left at a rate of 33.33% if they worked overtime. This suggests overtime is a more actionable and reliable predictor of flight risk than self-reported satisfaction scores — a useful distinction for HR teams deciding where to focus retention efforts.
